I love how fun Roald Dahl's writing is, and reading an autobiography in his style was very entertaining, especially considering he lived such an interesting life.

The book starts off as a continuation of where his childhood memories end, with him being a 22-year-old on a journey to East Africa to work for the Shell company. The first half focuses on his journey and his time there, and it is more lighthearted, funny, and adventurous. I think you can see how these experiences later affected his books.

Then the war breaks out, and the tone of the book changes. He joins the RAF and becomes a fighter pilot, going through training and fighting in the war. His accounts highlight how senseless and brutal war is.

He also talks about his thoughts on how an autobiography should be written in the beginning, saying:

**_"A life is made up of a great number of small incidents and a small number of great ones. An autobiography must therefore, unless it is to become tedious, be extremely selective, discarding all the inconsequential incidents in one's life and concentrating upon those that have remained vivid in the memory."_**

and he continues with a great example of it.
