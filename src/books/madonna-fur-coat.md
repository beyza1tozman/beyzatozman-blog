I loved this book so much. I love when I go into a book with not much expectation and it surprises me wonderfully. I wasn't reading as much as I wanted to in these past few months, didn't like or finish a lot of books this summer, but I finished this in two sittings. After that, I even continued reading books I had left half finished, so it really cured my book slump.

The book starts with an unnamed narrator looking for a new job in Ankara. He runs into his old classmate, who offers him a job at the firm he works at. He accepts and gets set in a room across the table from a man called Raif Efendi. He is a very quiet man, and with time the narrator gets curious about him and also becomes friends with him. Raif Efendi has a notebook in his desk drawer that he reads from time to time. We get to know the contents of that notebook and learn more about parts of Raif Efendi's life, and the majority of the book is what's written in that journal.

It really reads like a journal of a real person. All the characters felt very real with such well-written inner thoughts and worries. It is a very touching story that is written very beautifully.

I read this from my phone, then I also bought a physical copy and will definitely read it again in the future.
