The longest book I have ever read, and the longest I have ever taken to finish a book. _The Count of Monte Cristo_ has become one of my favorite books.

I started talking about this book to my friends and family when I first began reading it, and I still talk about it from time to time. Some friends say they are intimidated by how long it is, but I assure them that it hooks you right from the start and creates a feeling of "I wish it was longer."

Edmond Dantès is an incredible protagonist, and watching him take his revenge over the years makes you feel justified.

I like the ending and how things got concluded overall, but a few things I can note is that I wanted Danglars to have the worst ending, and him leaving with the lightest punishment made me a little bitter. That said, it was also really good to read about how Edmond felt unsure about the right he had to this revenge plan after what happened to Villefort and the people around him, ultimately sparing Danglars with a lesser punishment. I also didn't like, as many people note, how the relationship turned out between Edmond and Haydée.

I finished this book in about six months, which is a bit embarrassing. I read the first 200–300 pages very fast, then entered a very slow reading phase during the Rome chapters, which were the only parts where I got a little bored. After reaching Paris, I thoroughly enjoyed it again, but I continued reading at a very slow pace because I just couldn't find the time to sit and read. I understand the argument of "How can you not find time to read a book? Even with how busy life gets, time can be found." Even if that is true, for me, I feel like I can't enjoy a book while I am anxious. I only try to read when I have more free time and can truly appreciate it.

I am not really enjoying the other books I'm trying to start at the moment, and I am even jokingly considering rereading _The Count of Monte Cristo_ already.

I highly recommend this book to anyone, and I will definitely read and enjoy this again in the future!
